## 1. The answer first

**The skip is worth 2.40× to 3.11×, and a 27× rise in depth costs it about 5 % of that — not the
collapse arch §7 feared.** Every reading is above the prototype's 2.06×.

Median of three runs, each the cheapest of seven interleaved rounds, on an otherwise idle machine;
the range across the three is in brackets.

| a walk keeping one record in | tomato, **10.3 reads a record** | HG002, **280.0 reads a record** | what depth costs |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 10 | **2.513×** (2.486–2.675) | **2.399×** (2.386–2.426) | −4.5 % |
| 100 | **3.038×** (3.025–3.040) | **2.869×** (2.831–3.057) | −5.6 % |
| 1,000 | **3.111×** (3.066–3.120) | **2.927×** (2.897–2.933) | −5.9 % |

**The two depths are the spec's own two corners, confirmed by measurement rather than by
filename.** Spec `psp_record_encoding.md` §6 takes its chain-id figures at *11.4 reads* and *293
reads*; these corpora measure **10.3** and **280.0** reads a record.

## 2. Why the question existed, and what still keeps it open

A record on the wire is

```text
position offset | reference span | non-reference reads | body bytes | chain-id changes | body
```

and **`body bytes` does not reach the changes** — deliberately, so a reader that skips a body still
carries the live set forward (E3). **The skip therefore never avoids the chain-id changes**, and
those grow with depth: 0.432 bytes a position at 11.4 reads, **6.42 at 293**. So the head gets
dearer while the body the skip avoids does not, and arch §7 asked how much of the 2.06× survives.

**⚠ These corpora cannot close it, and the reason is exact.** ng cannot yet write a psp, so the
stores are built from a production `.psp`. **Production names about 3.4 % of the reads ng will
name** (owner's ruling, 2026-08-17), so the chain-id changes in these heads are a small fraction of
ng's.

**The bias is one-directional, which is what makes the reading worth having.** A bigger head makes
the skip worth *less*, never more. **So every figure above is an upper bound** on what ng will see
at the same depth.

What H5 therefore delivers is not a closed question but a much narrower one: **the skip is worth at
least 2.35× on production-shaped records, the erosion with depth is small rather than catastrophic
at this chain-id density, and the remaining unknown is one quantity — what the full column does to
the head.** Closing it needs an ng-written store, which is
[`run_streaming.md`](../../ng/spec/run_streaming.md)'s.

**No projection is offered.** Turning the spec's 6.42 bytes into a predicted ratio would need the
head's decode *time*, and what is measured here is bytes and wall clock, not the head's share of
either. An arithmetic guess would read like a measurement, and this milestone has already produced
enough of those.

## 3. ⚠ How the number was got, because two earlier versions of it were wrong

**This measurement moved under me twice, and both failures are now designed out of the harness.**

- **Three rounds was not enough.** Five repeats of one command gave 2.865 to 3.143 — an 8 % spread.
  At seven rounds the same command gave 2.806 to 2.901, a 3 % one. The default is seven, and the
  reason is in the constant's own doc.
- **Running the two arms in separate phases biased the ratio.** With all the full walks first and
  all the skipping walks after, the arms met different machine conditions: under a background build
  the same command gave 2.93, 4.41 and 3.07 on one corpus. **The arms are interleaved round by
  round now**, so each pair meets the same machine, and the numbers above were taken with nothing
  else running.

A ratio that moves by half between identical runs is not a measurement, and this milestone has
produced enough figures that were quoted before they were stable.

## 4. What the numbers say beyond the headline

- **The value rises as the pass gets more selective and then flattens**: 2.40 → 2.87 → 2.93 on
  HG002 as the predicate goes from one in ten to one in a thousand. The flat end is the head-only
  cost, so **decoding heads is about a third of a full walk** at both depths.
- **99.00 % of the body mass is skipped** at one in a hundred, on both corpora — the predicate is
  doing what it says.
- **A declining walk still yields every record**, asserted per run: it is not a filter (spec §6.2).
- **Mean body: 24.96 bytes on tomato, 39.55 on HG002.** A 27× rise in depth costs 58 % more body
  bytes, which is the record encoding doing its job.

## 6. Tests added

**One**, on the harness's one piece of logic: that a reading is **the cheapest of its rounds**,
wherever the cheapest falls. A slower round is a machine doing something else; taking the mean would
let one noisy round move the ratio the whole step turns on, and taking the first would make the
reading a single sample.

The run itself carries three guards that fail rather than report: that a declining walk yields the
same record count as a full one, that the predicate declined something, and that the declined
records had body bytes to skip.

## 8. Trade-offs and follow-ups

- **Wall clock on one machine**, the cheapest of seven interleaved rounds, three runs per cell.
  The ratio is what carries; the seconds are not comparable across machines.
- **The chain-id density is the whole caveat**, and it is not this plan's to remove.
- **One predicate shape.** `seen % keep_one_in == 0` declines by ordinal, not by anything in the
  head. A cohort's real first pass reads `non_reference_reads`, which correlates with body size —
  so a real pass would skip *smaller* bodies than these and be worth slightly less.
