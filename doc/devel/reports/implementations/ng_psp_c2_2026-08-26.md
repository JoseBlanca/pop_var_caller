# ng psp store — C2: the record head, and the skip

*Implementation report, 2026-08-26. Step C2 of
[`../../ng/impl_plan/psp_file_format.md`](../../ng/impl_plan/psp_file_format.md), branch
`ng-psp-encoding`. Design authority:
[`../../ng/spec/psp_file_format.md`](../../ng/spec/psp_file_format.md) §4.3 (what the head carries
and why each field is in it) and §4.5 (the manifest).*

---

## 1. Plan

**`position_offset | reference_span | non_reference_reads | body_bytes | body`, and a decoder that
takes the head and either builds the body or advances past it.**

What landed, all in [`src/ng/psp/record.rs`](../../../../src/ng/psp/record.rs):

- `HEAD_FIELDS` — the head's four fields, declared in the manifest **ahead of** the body's
  nineteen. `record_fields()` is now the whole record's twenty-three.
- `RecordEncoder` — writes a record as a head then a body, keeping the one scratch buffer a
  record needs between calls.
- `read_record_head` — reads the head, rebuilds the region, and hands back the body **bounded to
  exactly the length the head declared**. This is the skip.
- `decode_record` — both halves, plus the check that the head's declared length and the body's
  real shape agree.
- `RecordEncodeError` — the three records the head cannot describe.

## 2. Assumptions and departures

### 2.1 The head's fields are variable-length integers, and the measurement that would settle it is owed to D2

[Spec §4.3](../../ng/spec/psp_file_format.md) leaves fixed-width against variable-length to the
manifest, notes that a fixed width is quicker to read and "costs less than it looks after
compression — the four head fields together compressed to 0.077 bytes a record when measured on
their own", and records that the two have never been compared **in place**.
[Arch §7](../../ng/arch/psp_file_format.md) asks for the choice to be pinned "with a measurement at
implementation time, not by argument".

**That measurement cannot be taken here.** In place means after compression, and nothing compresses
until D2. So the head is variable-length — the encoding every other field uses, and the one that
costs least uncompressed — and **the sweep is owed to D2**, where a compressor exists. It is a
format change rather than a mere manifest one — this reader refuses a file declaring anything but
what it knows, so a build that switched would reject every psp written before it and vice versa.
**What makes deferring it cheap is that no psp exists yet**, not that the change is free; from
Milestone F it costs a version.

> **⚠ Corrected 2026-08-26 by [the review](../reviews/ng_psp_c2_2026-08-26.md).** This paragraph,
> the doc comment it describes and `PROJECT_STATUS.md` all said the switch would be "a manifest
> change, not a format change". It would not be, for the reason above. The deferral itself holds —
> the measurement genuinely needs a compressor — but its cost was understated.

### 2.2 The layout now covers the whole record, so C1's two names changed

C1 introduced `RecordBodyLayout` / `RecordBodyLayoutError` / `record_body_fields`, because the body
was all there was. The head is part of the record and its encodings are the manifest's to say
(§4.5), so there is **one** manifest, **one** layout and **one** field list over all twenty-three
fields: `RecordLayout`, `RecordLayoutError`, `record_fields`. The unknown-trailing-field machinery
is unchanged and now begins after twenty-three rather than after nineteen.

### 2.3 The head's non-reference read count is derived here, not supplied by a caller

`RecordEncoder::push` computes it from the record — a caller passing it in is a caller who can get
it wrong, and the record already knows.

**What it counts is narrower than "reads that disagree", and the tests say so.** Only observations
that spanned the whole locus are scored: a partial one's bases stop where its read's witness
stopped, so there is nothing to compare them against. The rich fixture is the zero case — its one
complete observation shows the reference's own bases — which is why a second record with two
complete observations, one of them a variant, is built in that test.

### 2.4 `decode_record` exists so the head-against-body check cannot be forgotten

The head declares how long the body is; the body reports how much of itself it used. Split across
two calls that is a comparison a caller can omit, and omitting it is silent. `decode_record` makes
it, and a disagreement is `Malformed { field: "body-bytes" }`.

### 2.5 The base a record's offset is measured from is the caller's to supply, and getting it wrong is silent

`RecordEncoder::push` and `read_record_head` both take `previous_position`: the block's own first
position for a block's first record, the previous record's start after that. Every running
difference restarts at a block boundary ([spec §3.2](../../ng/spec/psp_file_format.md)), and that
reset is what lets a reader start at any block. **A caller carrying the wrong base writes a file
that reads back at the wrong coordinates and does not fail** — so it is stated on both, and
Milestone D is where the base stops being a parameter and becomes the block's own state.

## 6. Tradeoffs and follow-ups

- **The head's encoding is unmeasured** (§2.1). To D2, with the compressor.
- **The base an offset is measured from is a parameter, and a wrong one is silent** (§2.5). D1 is
  where the block owns it.
- **What the head is worth at depth is still the open question the specs record.** The chain-id
  live-set changes ride in the head from Milestone E3 and grow with depth — 0.432 bytes a position
  at 11.4 reads a position, 6.42 at 293 — so the head grows while the skip's value shrinks, and how
  much of the 2.06× survives is H5's measurement.
- **C3 is the oracle this step's machinery exists for**: a decode that skips every other record and
  still matches a full decode on the ones it keeps.
