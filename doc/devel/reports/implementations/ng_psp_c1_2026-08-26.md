# ng psp store — C1: one record's body to bytes and back

*Implementation report, 2026-08-26. Step C1 of
[`../../ng/impl_plan/psp_file_format.md`](../../ng/impl_plan/psp_file_format.md), branch
`ng-psp-encoding`. Design authority:
[`../../ng/arch/psp_file_format.md`](../../ng/arch/psp_file_format.md) §2 (what ng's record holds
that the measuring prototype's did not) and
[`../../ng/spec/psp_record_encoding.md`](../../ng/spec/psp_record_encoding.md) §5 (which fields
may be stored approximately, and at what step).*

---

## 1. Plan

**One `SampleLocusObservations` encoded into a `Vec<u8>` and decoded back, exactly.** No
compression, no file, nothing read from outside the bytes — which is what makes the whole of
Milestone C testable in memory, and why the plan puts the correctness work here.

What landed, all in [`src/ng/psp/record.rs`](../../../../src/ng/psp/record.rs):

- `BODY_FIELDS` — the nineteen fields a body carries, in encoding order, each with its
  encoding. **One list**: `record_body_fields()` turns it into what a writer declares in the
  header's manifest, `RecordBodyLayout::from_manifest` checks a file's manifest against it,
  and the codec writes the fields in that order.
- `encode_body` — infallible, appends to a caller's buffer.
- `decode_body` — takes the bytes, the region (which lives in the head, not the body) and the
  file's layout; returns the record and **how many bytes it consumed**, which is the number
  C2's `body_bytes` gets checked against.
- `RecordBodyLayout` — built once per file from its manifest, used per record.
- `RecordLayoutError` (per file, at open) and `RecordDecodeError` (per record).

The head — `position_offset | reference_span | non_reference_reads | body_bytes` — is C2 and is
not written here.

## 2. Assumptions and departures

### 2.1 The reference bases are stored — the open question C1 had to close

[Spec psp_record_encoding §4](../../ng/spec/psp_record_encoding.md) leans to **not** storing
`reference_bases` and re-fetching them when a block is decoded, and says *"confirm before code"*
— the confirmation being a timed re-fetch inside a segment walk.
[Arch §7](../../ng/arch/psp_file_format.md) lists it as impl-time and notes nobody has timed it.

**They are stored, declared in the manifest as a length-prefixed byte string.** Three reasons:

- **The measurement that would settle it cannot be taken yet.** It needs a writer, a reader and
  a reference accessor in one walk; that is Milestone F at the earliest. C1's contract is an
  exact in-memory round trip, and a record that has to be handed a reference to come back is not
  one.
- **Dropping them later is a manifest change, not a format break.** The field list is declared
  per file and this reader is driven by it, so a later writer omits the field, declares the
  shorter list, and an old reader refuses the file with a message naming the missing field
  rather than misparsing it.
- **The invariant that would make them free does not hold.** If a record's reference bases
  always covered exactly its region, the length would be derivable from the head and cost
  nothing. They do not: `SampleLocusObservations` has a public `Box<[u8]>` field and the crate's
  own fixtures already build records with an empty one
  ([`src/ng/locus_generation/mod.rs:1106`](../../../../src/ng/locus_generation/mod.rs)). So the
  field carries its own length, and a length byte per record is the price.

**What it costs is not measured and is worth measuring at Milestone H**, in the same run that
would settle dropping the field: the allele sequences, the same kind of content, compressed to
0.32–0.34 bytes a record on both corners (spec §4), and the reference bases are longer on every
record a deletion widened.

### 2.2 The locus kind is stored, which the plan's field list does not name

C1's contract reads "every field except the chain ids", and lists the observations, their
sequences, `read_witness`, `read_group`, the support moments and the two counts. `kind` is a
field of the record and is not in that list; neither is `reference_bases`. **Both are encoded**,
because "every field except the chain ids" is the sentence that governs and an exact round trip
is the step's oracle. A tract's motif and its two flanks ride with the kind tag.

### 2.3 The summed log-error is a **zig-zag** variable-length integer

The manifest declares it `fixed-point` with 4,096 steps to the natural log, which is the step
`SummedLogError` rounds to and not a choice this writer makes (spec §5.1.1). The bytes are
zig-zag rather than plain, because the quantity is a sum of log error probabilities and every
probability's log is at most zero. The doc comment on `FieldEncoding::FixedPoint` said "written
as a varint"; it now says which kind and why. Zig-zag frames its bytes exactly as the unsigned
form does, so a reader walking past a fixed-point field it does not recognise still measures it
correctly.

**A file declaring any other step is refused rather than rescaled**, which is what keeps the
step the type's property.

### 2.4 A witnessed run is stored as its own start and length, not as a step from the run before

A step would be a byte smaller on a witness with holes and would depend on the runs arriving
sorted and non-overlapping — an invariant `WitnessedLocusPositions` does keep, but one this
encoder would then be silently wrong about if it stopped keeping it. Locus positions are at most
65,535, so an absolute start costs at most three bytes and on a real locus costs one. The
decoder rebuilds the set through `from_half_open_runs`, which canonicalises, so a damaged file
cannot produce a witness the type would refuse to mint.

### 2.5 Forward compatibility is delivered as "unknown fields come last, one per record"

The owner's ruling of 2026-08-26 is that the window's GC fraction and its mean coverage may be
added later — neither is computed anywhere in ng today — and that adding a per-record scalar must
be cheap and must not break a reader. **Every encoding in the closed set measures its own
length**: a variable-length integer ends at its own last byte, a fixed-width one is its declared
width, a byte string carries its length in front. So a reader meeting a field it does not know,
*after* every field it does know, walks past it and produces the record it always produced.

**The limit is stated where it bites**: the manifest carries no cardinality
([A1+A2 §2.8](ng_psp_a1_a2_2026-08-26.md)), so this works for a field that appears once per
record and not for a repeated one. A later writer adding a per-observation field must add a
version, not a trailing field.

### 2.6 The record codec is re-exported from `ng::psp`

`arch §4` fixes the caller-facing operations and does not name the codec. It is re-exported
anyway, beside the header vocabulary A1 already re-exported, because the module's own submodules
are `pub(crate)` and a codec reachable only from tests is one the compiler reports as dead until
Milestone D3 calls it.

**Nothing outside `src/ng/psp/` was touched**, and nothing in the frozen production tree. The
variable-length integer codec is production's, called and not copied
([`src/psp/varint.rs`](../../../../src/psp/varint.rs)); both integer readers go through one
place that turns a `VarintError` into this module's own, so "the bytes ran out here" has one
spelling.

**Two things are deliberately absent and both are stated in the code.** A record's chain ids are
dropped and come back empty — Milestone E — and the record's coordinate is not in the body at
all, because it rides in the head.

| §2.1's decision | `the_reference_bases_are_in_the_body_so_a_decode_needs_no_reference` — four reference bases over a one-base region, so nothing about the region says what they are |
| §2.2's decision | `an_str_record_round_trips_with_its_motif_and_its_flanks`, `a_repeat_bundle_record_round_trips` |

| §2.3 | `a_summed_log_error_round_trips_negative_and_at_its_extremes` (seven values, `i64::MIN` and `i64::MAX` among them); `a_manifest_that_declares_a_different_step_for_the_summed_log_error_is_refused` |

| §2.5 | `a_field_this_reader_does_not_know_is_walked_past_when_it_comes_last` — **all six encodings**, each with a body a later writer would have written, each asserting both the record and that the whole body was accounted for |
| the manifest and the codec being one decision | `the_manifest_a_writer_declares_is_the_one_this_reader_checks`; `a_manifest_that_renames_reorders_or_drops_a_field_is_refused` — **56 refusals**: each of the 19 fields renamed, each dropped, and each of the 18 adjacent pairs swapped; `a_manifest_that_ends_early_names_the_field_it_stopped_before` |
| damaged and hostile input | `a_body_cut_short_is_refused_at_every_cut_and_never_panics` — **75 cuts**, one per byte of the fixture's encoded body; `a_declared_count_larger_than_the_body_never_reaches_the_allocator`; `a_count_too_large_for_its_field_is_refused_rather_than_narrowed`; `an_unknown_locus_kind_is_refused`; `a_stored_motif_outside_the_repeat_period_range_is_refused` |
| the block reader's contract | `a_body_followed_by_more_bytes_stops_where_the_record_ends` — in a block the next record starts there |

Three of these exist because the failure they catch is silent rather than loud:

- **`a_declared_count_larger_than_the_body_never_reaches_the_allocator`.** A body of a few bytes
  declares 2<sup>64</sup>−1 observations, and separately 2<sup>64</sup>−1 witnessed runs. Both
  reserve against what is left of the body rather than against the declared count, so the test
  returns; if the declared count reached the allocator it would not.
- **`a_manifest_that_renames_reorders_or_drops_a_field_is_refused`.** Every one of those 56
  files decodes into plausible values rather than failing if the layout check is dropped: two
  adjacent variable-length integer fields swapped is two numbers exchanged, not an error.
- **`an_unknown_locus_kind_is_refused`.** A tag this reader does not know must not be read as
  the kind whose tag is next to it, or a file from a later writer has its repeat tracts turned
  into SNP candidates.

**The byte count in the truncation test was measured, not recalled**: the fixture's body encodes
to 75 bytes (a throwaway `println!` of `bytes.len()` on the fixture, removed again), so the loop
makes 75 refusals and one successful decode.

## 6. Tradeoffs and follow-ups

- **The reference bases cost a length byte a record and are unmeasured** (§2.1). To Milestone H,
  in the same run that would settle dropping them.
- **A field added later must be a per-record scalar** (§2.5). A repeated one needs the
  cardinality the manifest does not carry.
- **Nothing here is on a hot path yet.** `decode_body` allocates a `Box<[u8]>` per sequence and a
  `Vec` per record, which is what an in-memory round trip needs; whether the block reader wants
  to borrow from its rolling buffer instead is Milestone D3's question, and the spec's own
  reason for serialising by hand is that a generic serialiser cannot
  ([spec §4.6](../../ng/spec/psp_file_format.md)).
- **The body's field order was not chosen for compression.** It follows the struct, and the
  measurement that would justify moving anything needs a compressor, which is Milestone D2.
