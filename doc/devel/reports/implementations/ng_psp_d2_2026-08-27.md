# ng psp store — D2: compress a block with a capped look-back window

*Implementation report, 2026-08-27. Step D2 of
[`../../ng/impl_plan/psp_file_format.md`](../../ng/impl_plan/psp_file_format.md), branch
`ng-psp-encoding`. Design authority:
[`../../ng/spec/psp_file_format.md`](../../ng/spec/psp_file_format.md) §4.2 (the window is
declared and the reader honours it), §1 (why the two numbers are untied), §8 (the traps), and
[`../../ng/arch/psp_file_format.md`](../../ng/arch/psp_file_format.md) §7 (the measurement this
step owes).*

---

## 1. What the step is

**A block payload goes in; a whole on-disk block comes out.** `BlockCompressor` compresses one
block at a time with zstd's look-back window capped at what the file declares, and hands back a
four-byte length and then one zstd frame.

**The cap is the design in one parameter.** Without it zstd sizes its window from the data, so a
reader would have to hold a whole block's worth of history to resolve a back-reference — which
ties the block size to the reader's memory, and untying those two is what this format exists for.
With it, a block is as large as compression and a small index want, while what a reader holds is
the declared window and nothing more.

## 2. Decisions taken here

### 2.1 What stands in front of a frame: a byte count, and nothing else

The measuring prototype's framing carries the *uncompressed* length beside the compressed one.
This does not, and spec §8 says why: "a block header that carries its uncompressed length is a
temptation to allocate it". The four bytes that are here do the opposite job — they bound how
many compressed bytes belong to *this* block, so a reader feeding a decoder stops at the frame's
end without having to trust the frame to tell it.

**The same argument reaches inside the frame.** zstd will write the payload's length into the
frame header if asked; `ContentSizeFlag` is cleared, so it does not, and
`a_frame_says_nothing_about_how_large_it_inflates_to` is what keeps it cleared. That test was
added because the mutation turning the flag back on **passed every other test in the module** —
the trap the spec names is exactly the one the suite could not see.

### 2.2 The level is not declared in the file, and the window is

Both are the user's choices ([spec §1.2](../../ng/spec/psp_file_format.md)), and only one has to
travel. A reader configures its decoder from the window, so the manifest carries it; a reader
never needs the level, because a zstd frame carries everything a decoder needs. The level is
production's 9, kept because every byte figure the specs quote was measured at it.

### 2.3 The frame gets its own buffer, and the reason is a defect this step made

`compress_to_buffer` writes into the destination **from its start**, not from its end: zstd-safe's
`WriteBuf for Vec<u8>` hands zstd `as_mut_ptr()` and then `set_len`. So the first version reserved
four bytes for the length, compressed behind them — and zstd overwrote them, putting a length
where the frame's magic belongs. `a_block_round_trips_through_the_compressor_record_for_record`
failed on its first run with zstd reporting an unknown frame descriptor. The compressor keeps two
buffers now, and the reason is written at the field.

### 2.4 Not split into a `block/` directory yet

[The D1 review](../reviews/ng_psp_d1_2026-08-27.md) suggested naming the split now so D2 lands as
a sibling file. Deferred to **D3**, deliberately: splitting now would make D2's diff a large move
plus a small addition, which is harder to review than an addition, and the third concern — the
streaming reader — is the one that makes the seams obvious. `block.rs` is 2,247 lines with its
tests; `record.rs` is 4,222 and the crate keeps tests in-file.

## 3. The measurement arch §7 asked for, and it is decided

**Are the record head's four fields cheaper as variable-length integers or as fixed-width ones,
compared in place — after compression?** Spec §4.3 leaves the width to the manifest, records that
a fixed width is quicker to read and "costs less than it looks after compression", and says the
two have never been compared in place. Arch §7 asks for a measurement rather than an argument.
`examples/ng_psp_head_encoding.rs` is it.

**The answer is variable-length, on both samples and at every width tried.**

| | tomato SRR7279481 (7,687,686 records, 160 blocks, **10.25 reads a position**) | HG002 chr21 (74,623 records, 281 blocks, **279.99 reads a position**) |
|---|---:|---:|
| **varint — what ships** | **4.676** | **15.669** |
| fixed 4/4/4/4 (16 bytes) | 4.913 (+5.07 %) | 16.257 (+3.76 %) |
| fixed 4/2/2/4 (12 bytes) | 4.846 (+3.64 %) | 16.068 (+2.55 %) |
| fixed 2/1/2/2 (7 bytes) | 4.749 (+1.57 %) | *does not fit* — a position offset exceeds 65,535 |

Compressed bytes a record, at the 100 kb default grid and a 32 kB window.

**The spec's intuition was right and the conclusion still goes the other way.** A fixed-width head
*does* compress away almost all of what it adds: on tomato it costs 12 more uncompressed bytes a
record and 0.237 more compressed ones, so **compression removes 98 % of what fixed width adds**.
The 2 % that survives is 5.07 % of the whole file, because the file is only 4.676 bytes a record
to begin with. At 279 reads a position the file is larger and the same 12 bytes cost 3.76 %.

**And the narrow widths are not a real option.** The 7-byte arm is the cheapest fixed one on
tomato and it *cannot encode the human sample at all*: **15 of its records have a within-block
position offset over 65,535, the largest 90,467**, because its coverage is scattered rather than
contiguous. On tomato the widest such offset is 21,115 and none passes it, which is why the same
arm fits there. A manifest may declare narrow widths, but a writer that declares them cannot know
its data in advance — which is the whole reason the format's own head is variable-length.

*⚠ Both of the previous sentence's facts were originally quoted from spec §4.1 as "644 regions",
which the corpus does not reproduce (it gives 1,217 maximal runs of covered positions in 281
occupied 100 kb cells). The harness measures the offsets directly now, and prints them.*

**So the head stays as C2 wrote it, and arch §7's open item is closed.** Switching would have been
a format change, and it was free only while no psp existed; it is now decided rather than
deferred.

### 3.1 What the corpus is, and what it is not

**⚠ The depth range these two corpora cover is 10× to 280×, not 3× to 279×.** The harness
measures each corpus and prints it: the tomato accession is **10.25 reads a position** and HG002
chr21 is **279.99**. The "three reads a position" label this report first carried is the specs'
(§4.1, §4.3, which arch §7 already contradicts with 11.4) and is not a fact about this file. **So
the 3× end of the range this caller is committed to is covered by neither corpus** — the
conclusion below holds at 10× and at 280×, and thinner data has not been tried.

ng cannot yet write a psp, so the records come from a production `.psp` and are converted. Three
of the four head fields are exact — the position offsets, the spans and the non-reference read
counts are the real ones. The fourth, the body's byte count, is **approximate in both
directions**: ng's body carries a read witness and a read group production has no equivalent of,
and the chain ids are not encoded until Milestone E. So the *bodies* here are smaller than ng's
finished ones and the head's share of the file is overstated. What the comparison turns on — which
of two encodings of the same four values is cheaper — is unaffected, because both arms see the
same bodies.

**The harness reproduces the shipped cut rather than calling it**, because the fixed-width arm
needs a head `BlockBuilder` does not write. So it checks itself: the varint arm's block payloads
are compared byte for byte against `BlockBuilder`'s over the same records, and the run aborts if
they differ. **Falsified**: swapping two fields of the harness's own head makes it stop with
`block 0: the harness wrote 6195 bytes and the shipped builder 6195` — the same length, different
bytes, which is exactly the case a length comparison would have missed.

## 4. What the tests are

Ten new ones, taking `ng::psp::block` from 32 to 42 and `ng::psp` to 158. *(The fix commit takes `block` to 49.)* The load-bearing ones:

| test | the defect it exists for |
|---|---|
| `a_block_round_trips_through_the_compressor_record_for_record` | a frame that does not inflate to the payload — this caught §2.3 on its first run |
| `the_shipped_settings_cap_a_payload_larger_than_the_window` | the cap not reaching zstd **at the settings a writer ships with**. ⚠ The version D2 committed ran at the *minimum* window and level 1, where "one exponent less" is refused by zstd's own parameter check before the frame is looked at — so half of it could not fail, and the shipped configuration was untested. The review's Blocker |
| `a_frame_says_nothing_about_how_large_it_inflates_to` | spec §8's temptation to allocate a whole block |
| `damage_to_a_frame_is_refused_or_harmless_and_never_silently_different` | a damaged block decoding into *different* records instead of being refused. ⚠ D2's version claimed every damaged byte is refused; that holds for whole-byte inversion and not for single bits — 9 flips of 552 are accepted and inflate unchanged. The property that matters, and the one now asserted, is that none inflates to anything different |
| `compressing_a_block_does_not_depend_on_the_blocks_before_it` | a writer whose bytes depend on how its blocks were scheduled, which is goal 5's byte-identity check made meaningless |
| `a_blocks_length_prefix_says_where_the_next_block_starts` | a length that does not find the next block |
| `a_look_back_window_outside_what_zstd_takes_is_refused` | a window `header.rs` refuses in a manifest being acted on here |

**A frame's declared window can only be tested on a payload larger than the window**, or zstd
narrows the frame's own declaration to fit and the test passes for a reason that says nothing.
The fixture is built from a measured record size to be past 2^10 rather than assumed to be.

## 7. What D3 inherits

- **A whole on-disk block**, and `compressed_block_bytes` to find where the next one starts
  without touching a frame.
- **The window a decoder must be configured with**, from the same manifest field the compressor
  caps at — a link the fix commit makes real with `BlockCompressor::from_manifest`; at D2 the
  compressor took a bare `u8` and nothing tied the two together.
- **⚠ A frame's own declared window is often *narrower* than the file's**, because zstd shrinks it
  to fit a small payload. Spec §4.2's "a mismatch is a corruption worth detecting" has to be read
  as `≤`, or D3 rejects nearly every block of every file this writer produces.
- **A test-side streaming decompressor** in this module's tests — a `DCtx` with `WindowLogMax`
  set, growing its output rather than sizing it from the frame. It is not the reader D3 builds,
  which streams into a rolling buffer, but it is the seam that proves the window is honoured.
