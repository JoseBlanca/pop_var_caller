# ng psp store — B3: the summed log-error becomes an integer, and B1/B2 have no subject in ng

*Implementation report, 2026-08-26. Milestone B of
[the psp store plan](../../ng/impl_plan/psp_file_format.md), branch `ng-psp-encoding`. Design
authority: [`psp_record_encoding.md` §5.1.1](../../ng/spec/psp_record_encoding.md).*

---

## 1. What Milestone B is for, and what landed

The milestone's purpose is one sentence from the plan: **rounding where the value is computed means
direct mode and psp mode see the same number**, so the store keeps an integer it was handed rather
than approximating anything. That matters because
[`run_streaming.md` §1.2](../../ng/spec/run_streaming.md) makes "the same VCF from direct mode and
from psp mode" the oracle everything else is checked against, and a float rounded *in the file* would
degrade that check from *identical* to *within a tolerance*.

**B3 landed. B1 and B2 could not, and the reason is not a difficulty — it is that the quantities they
name do not exist in ng.** §3 below.

## 2. B3 — `q_sum` is a `SummedLogError`

[`SummedLogError`](../../../../src/ng/types.rs) holds the summed per-read log-error as **an integer
count of steps of 1/4,096 of a natural log**. `SequenceObservation.q_sum` is one; the three producers
round their finished `f64` sum once when they build the observation; consumers that need a float ask
for `.nats()`.

**Rounded once at the end of a sum, not once per read**, and this was a real choice. Rounding each
read's own term and adding the integers would be exactly order-independent, but it accumulates up to
half a step per read — about 0.0012 natural logs at 300 reads a position, **ten times** the error of
rounding the finished sum. The ordering difference the type exists to absorb lives in the last bits
of an `f64`, far below one step, so summing in floating point and rounding once is both more accurate
and sufficient. The doc comment carries this.

### 2.1 What it deleted, which is more than it changed

**A whole class of fragility in the parity differential is gone.** `q_sum` was an `f64` running sum,
and `f64` addition is not associative, so ng's walk and production's — which add the same terms in
different orders — landed an ulp apart. That differential had already been through two designs
because of it: a fixed nine-decimal grain, abandoned when HG002 at 300× produced a locus at
`q_sum ≈ −3360.39` where two accumulations differed by one grain, and then a relative tolerance.

Measured on the synthetic differential after the change: **the count of loci that agree only because
of the tolerance is 0 — of 145,108 in the anchor and of 163,091 in the census.** Before B3 the
recorded figure was 103 of 216,203. The rounding absorbs the ordering difference outright, which is
exactly what [spec §5.1.1](../../ng/spec/psp_record_encoding.md) predicted it would do.

Three consequences, all in the code:

- `LocusEvidence` now sums **step counts**, so its fold is exact and its `&mut f64` accumulator is
  gone.
- The tolerance is now **whole steps, one per observation folded**, replacing a relative one. A
  relative tolerance is the wrong shape for this: the difference to admit is a fixed 2.4 × 10⁻⁴, and
  at `q_sum ≈ −3` a 1e-9 relative allowance is 3 × 10⁻⁹ — five orders too small. **Measured by
  trying it**: the whole differential failed at seed `0x5eed0001`, case 3, locus 13, on ng's
  `−14.900146` against production's `−14.899902`, exactly one step apart.
- **The differential rounds production's side too**, because it projects production's records into
  ng's observation type before comparing. So both sides sit on the grid, and a value within an ulp of
  a step boundary can fall either way — which is why the allowance is one whole step and not half.

### 2.2 And a state that is no longer representable

A not-a-number error sum once came back through `f64::max` as *the most confident read the model can
express* — a confident wrong answer with nothing failing. An integer has no such value.
`from_nats` says what it does with one instead of leaving it to `as`.

## 3. B1 and B2 have no subject in ng — a plan/code contradiction

**The plan asks for the window's GC fraction and the window's mean coverage to "become an integer
type". Neither quantity is computed anywhere in ng.** Searched: `gc_fraction`, `GcFraction`,
`mean_coverage`, `window_gc`, `per_window` across `src/ng/` — no match. Both exist **only in
production**, which is frozen: [`src/sample_summary/coverage.rs:283`](../../../../src/sample_summary/coverage.rs)
holds `gc_fraction: f32`, and [`src/paralog/coverage_model.rs`](../../../../src/paralog/coverage_model.rs)
consumes it.

They are not an oversight. They are the per-window statistic
[`parameter_prepass_joint_fit.md` §2.2](../../ng/spec/parameter_prepass_joint_fit.md) **proposes** for
the duplicated-site class — described there as "the first new accumulator step 4 would add" — and
that accumulator has not been built.

**So nothing can round today where nothing computes.** Two ways forward, and this is the milestone's
stop-and-ask:

1. **Mint the two newtypes now with no producer and no consumer**, so the accumulator cannot get it
   wrong when it is built.
2. **Record the requirement against the accumulator instead**, and mint the types with it.

**Recommendation: (2).** A type nothing constructs is the speculative generality this project's own
reviews flag, and the property Milestone B exists to protect — the two routes agreeing — is vacuous
while there is no producer, no consumer and no psp field. What is worth having now is that whoever
builds the accumulator knows the rule, which is a line in that spec rather than a type in `types.rs`.

## 4. The oracle the plan asks for cannot be run yet

The plan names B3's oracle precisely: *"the calling comparison — the same records called before and
after, with the count of changed genotypes and the movement in quality scores reported."*

**ng cannot call a genotype yet.** There is no ng subcommand in
[`src/pop_var_caller/cli.rs`](../../../../src/pop_var_caller/cli.rs) and no example that runs a locus
through to a call; the calling loop has its vocabulary, its genotype prior, its candidate selection
and its read likelihoods, and nothing that closes the loop. So there is no *before* and *after* to
compare and no genotype to count.

**What was measured instead, and it is weaker in a way worth naming** — it bounds the perturbation
rather than its effect on a call:

| what | measured |
|---|---|
| the largest a value can move | half a step, 1.22 × 10⁻⁴ natural logs — asserted by a test over values from 0 to −3,360 |
| what that is worth in the likelihood term it enters | 0.024 %, from [spec §5.1.1](../../ng/spec/psp_record_encoding.md)'s own table — **quoted, not measured here** |
| ng against production, on the synthetic differential | every one of 145,108 anchor loci agrees within one step per observation; 0 needed the allowance |
| ng against ng, across the two column paths | exact — the two paths now produce the same integer, where before they needed a tolerance |

**The genotype comparison should be run when the calling loop closes**, and this is the reason to
remember it: a step that is wrong by a factor is a wrong genotype and not a crash, which is why the
plan gave B3 its own commit.

### ⚠ A correction to the A3 commit, and to how I read a test log

**The A3 commit message says `cargo test --lib --bins --tests --examples` was "green". It was
not.** `examples/ng_generic_loci_dump.rs` was already failing 11 of its 12 tests at that commit, each
with `NotFound { path: "/tmp/.tmpXXXXXX/ref.fa.repeats.parquet" }` — the example builds a reference
in a temporary directory and the repeat catalog beside it is not there when the dump runs.

**The reason I reported it green is worth more than the fact.** I filtered the run's output with a
pattern that matched `^test result: ok. [0-9]{3,}` — which shows the library's line and hides every
failing target, because a failing suite's line does not start with `ok`. The A1+A2 runs used a
pattern that did include `FAILED` and showed none, which is why the failure looked new.

**It is not this step's, and it is not A3's either.** Verified by setting this step's diff aside and
re-running the example at the A3 commit: the same 11 tests fail identically. Reported here rather
than amended into the commit, and it is **not fixed** — the example is a probe over real data and
what is wrong with its fixture is its own to chase.

## 7. Tradeoffs and follow-ups

- **The downstream calling types keep their `f64` `q_sum`** — `SupportedAllele`, `GenericObservation`
  and the cohort merge's accumulators. They are derived inside one route and never cross the psp
  boundary, so both routes start from the same integer and reach the same float. Making them
  integers too would be a separate change with no oracle behind it.
- **`PVC_PARITY_CASES` does not reach the container.** `scripts/dev.sh` forwards only `CARGO_*` and
  `RUST*`, so a soak run that sets it silently gets the default fixture — which is the exact trap
  that script's own comment warns about for `RUSTFLAGS`. The pre-B3 figures quoted at
  `PVC_PARITY_CASES=3000` cannot be reproduced through the wrapper. **Not fixed here**; it is
  tooling, not this step.
