# ng psp store — G2: the head-driven skip as a public iterator

*Implementation report, 2026-08-28. Step G2 of
[`../../ng/impl_plan/psp_file_format.md`](../../ng/impl_plan/psp_file_format.md), branch
`ng-psp-encoding`. Design authority: [spec §6.2, §4.3](../../ng/spec/psp_file_format.md),
[arch §4.1](../../ng/arch/psp_file_format.md).*

---

## 1. What the step is

**A walk that builds only the records a predicate asks for.**

```rust
psp.records_where(|head| head.non_reference_reads > 0)?   // the whole file
psp.records_from(at)?.only_where(|head| …)                // from a coordinate
```

Every record still opens with the head of spec §4.3, so the walk hands the caller the head and
the caller says whether it wants the body. A record the predicate declines costs its head and a
pointer advance; its body is never decoded.

Milestone D3 already had the mechanism — `BlockStream::next_record_where` — and what was missing
was the public shape and a file to exercise it through.

## 2. Decisions taken here

### 2.1 It is not a filter, and the doc says so first

**Every record of every block arrives, in order.** What the predicate decides is whether
[`StreamedRecord::record`] is `Some`. A caller reading `records_where` as a filter would take the
walk's length for the number of records it kept — so the ⚠ is the first thing in the type's doc,
and `a_declined_record_arrives_with_its_head_and_no_body` pins both counts with a fixture where
they differ (40 heads, 20 bodies).

### 2.2 The predicate is a builder on the walk, not a fourth entry point

Spec §6.2 names `records_where`; arch §4.1 gives it a `SelectiveIter` of its own. Taken literally
that is one predicate-taking entry point per starting point — three more methods, and the cohort's
real shape is the one the spec does not write: **one region of every sample, with a predicate**.

So the predicate is [`RecordIter::only_where`], which any walk can be turned into, and
`PspReader::records_where` is the whole-file case of it. Two types, four entry points, one
predicate mechanism.

### 2.3 One place a record is stepped

`RecordIter::step` takes the predicate and classifies the failure; `Iterator for RecordIter` is
`step(|_| true)` and `SelectiveIter::next` is `step(&mut self.want)`. **The full walk and the
selective one cannot drift**, and the error classification G1's review made exhaustive is written
once. `a_selective_walk_refuses_a_wrecked_block_the_same_way` holds that from outside.

### 2.4 `RecordHead` reaches `reader.rs` through `walk`

`records_where`'s bound names `RecordHead`, and `reader.rs` may not name the record module —
`the_opener_cannot_reach_any_block_decoding_code` forbids it, and G1's review widened the list to
the braced import forms. So `walk.rs` re-exports the type, with the reason on the re-export. A
file that may not decode a record has no business importing the decoder; naming the head in a
signature is not that, and the indirection says which of the two is happening.

## 4. Tests added

**Seven.** The three that carry the most:

**`the_records_a_predicate_keeps_match_a_full_walk`** — Milestone C3's property through the file:
every difference a body carries restarts at the record, so a skipped body strands nothing. The
records kept by a walk taking every third are compared field for field against the same positions
of a full walk. **This is the failure the head exists to avoid and it is silent** — the records
after a skipped one decode plausibly and wrong.

**`the_live_set_is_exact_after_a_declined_record`** — why the chain-id changes ride in the head
and not in the body (spec psp_record_encoding §6). The fixture's middle record is declined and is
where one id departs and another arrives; the live set still moves, so a caller keeping one record
in a hundred can derive the residual observation at the ones it keeps.

**`a_predicate_that_declines_everything_still_reads_every_block`** — the bytes arrive either way.
Skipping saves building the record, not decompressing it: a block comes out of zstd sequentially
and there is nothing to seek past. 40 records, no bodies, all 8 blocks opened.

The rest: a declined record arrives with its head and no body, with the two counts differing;
spec §6.2's own predicate — the cohort's first pass, `non_reference_reads > 0` — offers all 40
heads and keeps none on a fixture whose reads all agree with the reference; a walk from a
coordinate takes a predicate; and a selective walk refuses a wrecked block in the same class,
naming the same block.

**Four defects injected, four caught:**

| defect | caught by |
|---|---|
| the predicate is ignored | 6 |
| the predicate is inverted | 5 |
| a full walk builds no bodies | 10 |
| a selective walk counts no blocks | 1 |

⚠ **The harness misreported the third as a survivor**, because its "0 failed" test matched the
`10 failed` in the line it was reading. The line it printed showed the truth. The check is now
`; 0 failed`.

## 6. Trade-offs and follow-ups

- **What the skip is worth is still the prototype's number.** Spec §4.3's 2.06× — 0.141 s against
  0.29 s over 7.69 M records of a tomato accession, keeping one record in a hundred — was measured
  on the measuring program's reader, not on this one. **H5 times this reader**, on the
  279-reads-a-position sample as well as on tomato, and the chain-id changes ride in the head and
  grow with depth (0.432 bytes a position at 11.4 reads, 6.42 at 293), so the head grows while the
  skip's value shrinks. How much of the 2.06× survives is an open question arch §7 records.
- **`records_from_block(n)?.only_where(…)` works too** and has no test of its own; the coordinate
  case covers the composition.
