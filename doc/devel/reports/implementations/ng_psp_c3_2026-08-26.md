`ng-psp-encoding`. Design authority:
[`../../ng/spec/psp_file_format.md`](../../ng/spec/psp_file_format.md) §4.3 ("Why a skippable body

## 1. Plan

**Every difference the body carries restarts at the record, so a skipped body strands nothing.**
The plan gives the oracle: *a decode that skips every other record and still matches a full decode
on those it keeps*, and marks the step its own commit because "getting this wrong is the failure
the head exists to avoid, and it is silent — the records after a skipped one decode plausibly and
wrong."

**This step adds no encoding and changes no bytes.** What it adds is the oracle, a fixture large
and varied enough for the oracle to have an inside, and a statement in the code of the property
being protected. That is the whole of it, and §2.1 says why that is the right amount of work.

## 2. Assumptions and departures

### 2.1 The property holds by construction today, and the oracle exists so that it keeps holding

**No field of ng's record body is coded as a difference from another record**, and none ever was:
C1 encoded every one absolutely. So C3 could not find a bug — there was none to find — and a report
that stopped there would be describing a test that cannot fail.

**It can fail, and that was checked rather than assumed.** The defect was injected: one body field
— `reads-without-observation` — coded as a step from the previous record, on *both* sides, so that a
full walk still round-trips. Under it, **six tests failed and sixty-four passed**, and the ones that
passed are the point: every plain round-trip test, including the byte-for-byte golden one, is blind
to this. The six that failed are the four added here, C2's miniature skip test, and one C1 test
that decodes the same body twice.

**Why the property is worth a commit when nothing threatens it yet.** Two things arriving later do:
Milestone E gives each observation a chain-id exception list, which the spec requires to be measured
from zero rather than from the record before ([spec §4.3](../../ng/spec/psp_file_format.md)); and
the window's mean coverage, if it is ever added, is the field production codes as a difference from
the previous record — spec psp_record_encoding §7 names it as a trap by name. The oracle is what
those changes will be met by.

### 2.2 The guarantee is the signature, not a convention, and the code now says so

`encode_record_body` and `decode_record_body` take **no running state and are given none**. There is
no parameter through which a difference from another record could arrive, so the property is a
property of the shape rather than of anyone's discipline. That is now written on both, together
with the contrast that makes it meaningful: **the head is the half that carries state**, which is
why the chain ids' live-set changes go there and their exception lists stay in the body.

### 2.3 The fixture is twelve records, not three

Three records have no inside: every skip pattern over them is "the first", "the last" or "the
middle". Twelve gives patterns that skip runs, and the fixture varies what the review of C2 showed
the earlier one did not — spans of 1 to 12 bases, generic and tract and bundle kinds, records with
no observations and one at 300 reads a position whose head fields need more than a byte each, and
**gaps that are never the spans**, so a reader that took the distance to the next record for this
record's width fails on the first pair.

## 4. Tests

**Five new, `cargo test --lib ng::psp::record` now runs 70** (2 head-type, 37 body, 26 head and
skip, 5 here).

| what it pins | test |
|---|---|
| **the oracle the plan names** | `a_walk_that_skips_records_matches_a_full_decode_on_the_ones_it_keeps` — six patterns over twelve records, including skipping the first, the last, all but one, and all of them |
| **the strongest form of it** | `a_skipped_records_body_can_be_anything_and_the_kept_records_are_unchanged` — every skipped body overwritten with `0xff`, and every kept record still exact. If a body ever carried a difference from an earlier record, this is what fails, and the walk would still consume the block exactly, because the *head* is what says how far to advance |
| the body as a pure function of its own slice | `a_body_decodes_the_same_alone_as_it_does_after_every_record_before_it` — each body decoded from its own bytes with no earlier record read, **in reverse order**, so no state could have accumulated even by accident |
| the contrast that makes the design legible | `the_head_carries_state_across_records_and_the_body_carries_none` — a body may be skipped; a head may not, and a stale base reads without error |
| every pattern rather than six | `any_skip_pattern_builds_exactly_the_records_it_keeps` — a property over all 4,096 skip masks of a twelve-record block |

**The oracle was falsified before it was trusted.** With `reads-without-observation` coded as a
step from the previous record on both sides: `test result: FAILED. 64 passed; 6 failed`. Reverted,
and the file verified identical to the pre-injection copy.

> **⚠ Corrected 2026-08-26 by [the review](../reviews/ng_psp_c3_2026-08-26.md), which found the
> oracle could not fail in two other directions and three claims here wrong.** The falsification
> above is real and three agents reproduced it — but it was the *only* defect the oracle was tested
> against, and the two beside it were both invisible: a harness that built nothing left all 70 tests
> green, and the fixture carried no chain ids, so a correct Milestone E and an incorrect one gave
> identical results. Both are closed and the oracle re-falsified three ways; see
> [the fixes](../reviews/fixes_applied_ng_psp_c3_2026-08-26.md). **The wrong claims were mine about
> my own work**: the property test ran about 250 of the 4,096 masks rather than all of them (it is
> exhaustive now); `0xff` is not "a byte the encoder never writes" — it writes it eighteen times in
> this very run, and the true statement is that no body can *end* on it; there are three records at
> three hundred reads a position, not one; and six tests fail under the injection, not one. **And a
> proptest seed recorded during the injection was committed**, where it would have replayed for ever
> describing a failure that never happened; it is deleted.

## 6. Tradeoffs and follow-ups

- **The oracle is per-block, because there are no blocks yet.** It walks a run of records in one
  buffer. Milestone D5's restart-equals-sequential is the same property one level up, and it is
  where a running difference that failed to reset *at a block boundary* would be caught — this
  step cannot see that class.
- **Milestone E has to keep this.** The chain ids' exception lists go in the body and must be
  measured from zero; their live-set changes go in the head. These tests are what E will be met by,
  and `a_skipped_records_body_can_be_anything_and_the_kept_records_are_unchanged` is the one that
  will fail if E puts the wrong half in the wrong place.
