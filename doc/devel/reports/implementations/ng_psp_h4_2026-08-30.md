# ng psp store — H4: what an open sample costs, measured

*Implementation report, 2026-08-30. Step H4 of
[`../../ng/impl_plan/psp_file_format.md`](../../ng/impl_plan/psp_file_format.md), branch
`ng-psp-encoding`. Design authority: [spec §5.1, §5.2, §1.1](../../ng/spec/psp_file_format.md).*

---

## 1. The answer first

**On a human reference an open sample costs 480 kB, against a 500 kB budget. It fits, with 4 % to
spare — and three quarters of it is not the reader.**

| | per open sample | what it is |
|---|---:|---|
| hg002, 2,580 contigs | **480.3 kB** | the whole cost |
| — of which, before a block is touched | **357.4 kB** | the header, almost all of it the contig list |
| — of which, the reader itself | **122.9 kB** | two 16 kB buffers, the decoder, the record being built |
| tomato, 13 contigs | **107.9 kB** | the whole cost |
| — of which, before a block is touched | **6.7 kB** | |
| — of which, the reader itself | **101.1 kB** | |

Least-squares over sample counts 1 to 5,000, **R² = 0.99999** and better on every fit.

**The reader's own cost is 123 kB on a human reference and 101 kB on tomato** — near enough the
same on two corpora whose depth and contig count differ by two orders of magnitude, which is
exactly what §1.1 claims and the evidence for it.

**What is not near enough the same is the header.** 357 kB a sample on hg002 against 7 kB on
tomato, and the difference is 2,580 contigs against 13. **74 % of the human per-sample cost is a
contig list that is identical in every sample of the cohort.**

## 2. What was measured, and how

`examples/ng_psp_open_cost.rs` opens the same store `N` times, advances every reader one record per
round in lockstep — the shape a cohort merge reads in — and reports peak resident from `VmHWM`.

**⚠ One `N` per process, and that is not a convenience.** `VmHWM` is a high-water mark for the life
of the process. Two sample counts measured in one process would both report the larger, the slope
would come back zero, and the one claim the format exists to make would pass by construction.

**Opening the same store N times** is what the measuring prototype did for the same question: a
reader's buffers are the same size whatever they carry, so this gives the multiplier without
needing the samples to differ. It is a fact about the reader's cost, **not** about the variety of a
real cohort.

Both stores are cut on the **settled 100 kb grid**, written by `examples/ng_psp_parity.rs`, which
gained a `--grid-bp` flag for this — closing a Minor the H1 review raised, that taking a
measurement at another grid meant editing and rebuilding.

Measured: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 62, 125, 250, 500, 1,000 and **5,000** open samples. The 5,000-sample
figure is **2.40 GB, measured rather than extrapolated**.

## 3. Where the 500 kB budget actually stands

- **It is met.** 480.3 kB against 500 kB.
- **The headroom is 19.7 kB, or 4 %**, and it is not headroom in the reader — it is whatever is
  left after the contig list.
- **On a reference with more contigs it would not be met.** The cost is linear in the contig count:
  2,580 contigs cost 357 kB, so about 138 bytes a contig retained per open sample. A reference with
  3,700 contigs would put the budget over on the header alone.
- **Sharing the contig list across a cohort's open samples would take the human figure from 480 kB
  to about 123 kB.** Every sample in a run is called against one reference, so the list is the same
  object N times over. This is the largest single memory lever the store has and it is not in any
  plan; it belongs with [`run_streaming.md`](../../ng/spec/run_streaming.md), which owns the run
  objects.

## 4. Against the spec's own figures

Spec §5.2 quotes **257 kB** per open sample at 16 kB buffers, and §5.4 quotes **338 kB** — both
measured on the **prototype**, over production's records, and both are reader-only numbers: the
prototype's store has no contig list to retain. Against those, ng's reader at **123 kB** is
cheaper, not dearer.

**So the two figures are not comparable as they stand**, and the specs should not be read as
predicting 480 kB. What changed is not the reader but that ng's header carries the reference's
whole contig list and every open sample keeps a copy.

⚠ **§5.3's 190 kB floor is not visible here either.** It attributes about 190 kB per open sample to
zstd's own context, and the reader's whole cost is measured at 123 kB. Either the floor moved with
the decoder's configuration or the figure did not survive the port; **this is not resolved**, and
it is recorded rather than explained away.

## 6. Tests added

**Two**, on the harness's one piece of logic: that the peak is read from `VmHWM` and not `VmRSS`.
They differ by three characters and by everything that matters — one is the peak for the life of
the process, the other the size right now — and reading the second would make every sample count
report whatever the process happened to be holding, turning the slope into noise.

## 8. Trade-offs and follow-ups

- **`VmHWM` is Linux.** The dev container and CI are Linux; there is no macOS reading here.
- **One store per corpus, opened N times.** A real cohort's samples differ in size and in how far
  apart their records sit; what this measures is the multiplier.
- **1,000 rounds per reader** — past the first block, which is where a reader's buffers reach full
  size. The figure is reported with every reading so it is never quoted without it.
- **The contig-list finding is the actionable one**, and it is not this plan's to fix.
