# Code Review: ng psp D4 and D5 — the restartable parse, and the per-block reset

**Date:** 2026-08-27
**Reviewer:** rust-code-review skill (orchestrator), eight category checklists across three
sub-agents, each in its own git worktree
**Scope:** `4258c4b6..3a278a84` on `ng-psp-encoding` — three commits: the owner's two cut-rule
rulings (`2d058632`), D4 (`4cc7fb9b`), D5 (`3a278a84`)
**Status:** Request-changes

---

## 1. Scope

- **What was reviewed:** the diff of the three commits above, and the code they touch — the
  reader's retry arm, the per-block reset on both sides, the new record ceiling, and the tests
  and reports that go with them.
- **In-scope:** [src/ng/psp/block.rs](../../../../src/ng/psp/block.rs), the re-export line in
  [src/ng/psp/mod.rs](../../../../src/ng/psp/mod.rs), `record.rs`'s `PerBlockState`, and the two
  implementation reports plus `PROJECT_STATUS.md`, whose numbers were re-derived rather than read.
- **Deliberately out of scope:** the frozen production tree `src/psp/`; D1–D3, reviewed at their
  own commits; the two example targets that fail on a missing fixture (§3).
- **Categories dispatched:** all eight, paired onto three agents.

**What the three did.** **21 mutations** applied and run, **1,523,400 fuzzed inputs** across three
corpora, and a re-derivation of every number in the three commit messages, the two reports and the
status entry.

## 2. Verdict

**Request-changes. No Blocker, nine Majors, and one mutation that survived the whole suite.**

**The survivor is the new ceiling, measured against the wrong thing.** `pump` refuses when the
rolling buffer has grown as far as the reader allows; written against the buffer's **capacity**
instead of what it **holds**, all 81 tests pass — and a well-formed 2,100,075-byte block of three
700,000-base records is refused with *"a record needs more than the 1048576 bytes this reader
allows one to hold"*. The ceiling was added to bound a *corrupt* block's cost, and whether it also
binds on a *valid* one was untested in both directions: **no reader test decoded a block past the
ceiling at all**, though at the shipped 100 kb block size a fully covered block is about 1.76 MB
decompressed, so that is the ordinary case rather than an exotic one.

**And the type that makes the per-block reset unforgettable cannot hold the field it was built
for.** D5's report says Milestone E's chain-id difference belongs in `BlockCursor`, where a new
field is a compile error at the reset. Both per-block types derive `Copy`, and E's difference is a
live *set* — a collection. Measured, the first thing the compiler says to whoever adds it is
`error[E0204]: the trait Copy cannot be implemented for this type`, and the cheapest way past that
is to put the field on `BlockStream`, where it compiles, passes all 197 `ng::psp` tests, and is
never reset.

**The reader is otherwise sound under 1.52 million fuzzed inputs**: no panic, no hang, and over
400 inflation bombs decompressing to as much as 67,108,864 bytes the reader's own resident size
grew by **1,200,128 bytes** — the ceiling plus its read chunk. That is what the ceiling was added
for and it works.

## 4. Top 3 priorities

1. **R2 — the ceiling is one token from refusing valid production files**, and nothing would
   notice: no reader test decodes a block past it.
2. **RS1 — `BlockCursor` is `Copy`**, so the one field the whole refactor-safety argument was
   built for is the one that cannot live where the reset is.
3. **E1 — the refusal prints its own limit twice** and never the record's size, so *"raise the
   knob"* arrives with no number to raise it to.

## 5. Findings

### Major

**R1: [block.rs:1113](../../../../src/ng/psp/block.rs) — nothing reads back a block bigger than the ceiling, and that is the shipped block size**
**Categories:** reliability. **Confidence:** High. The largest block any reader test meets is
210,894 bytes; a fully covered 100 kb block is about 1.76 MB. Measured, the `capacity()` mutation
passes all 81 and refuses a well-formed 2 MB block — the survivor in §2.

**RS1: [block.rs:1195](../../../../src/ng/psp/block.rs) — `BlockCursor` and `PerBlockState` are `Copy`, and Milestone E's difference is a collection**
**Categories:** refactor_safety. **Confidence:** High. `E0204` before `E0063`; the field then
compiles on `BlockStream` and 197 tests pass with it never reset. **D5's report §6 asserts the
opposite** and needs correcting.

**E1: [block.rs:1747](../../../../src/ng/psp/block.rs) — the refusal reports the allowance twice**
**Categories:** errors. **Confidence:** High. `held_bytes` can only ever equal `allowed_bytes` —
the buffer doubles and the check runs before the growth, so `len` meets the ceiling exactly. Over
1,523,400 fuzzed inputs an added equality assertion never fired. The rendered message reads *"a
record needs more than the 1048576 bytes this reader allows one to hold; it had 1048576"*.

**D1: [block.rs:1113](../../../../src/ng/psp/block.rs) — the ceiling is documented as a knob and there is no knob**
**Categories:** defaults. `BlockStream::new` takes a source and a manifest; the ceiling is a
`pub const` read straight out of `pump`. The third reader budget in this type with no way in — the
D3 review recorded the first two.

**D2: [block.rs:1107](../../../../src/ng/psp/block.rs) — the measurement justifying 1 MiB does not reproduce, and the cohort side is priced at the bottom of the range**
**Categories:** defaults. "Roughly 30 kB" measures **18,292 bytes**; at a 150-base span the same
depth gives 48,693. And a thousand samples is the bottom of the committed range: at three thousand,
1 MiB each is **3.07 GB** against spec §1.1's 1.5 GB.

**X1: [block.rs:4605](../../../../src/ng/psp/block.rs) — `a_refill_inside_a_block_head_is_retried` tests something the design makes unreachable**
**Categories:** extras, smells — **two agents.** A reader clears its buffer at a block start and
zstd emits an internal block whole, so a refill lands *before* a head and never inside one.
Measured over 108,746 head restarts: **the largest partial head ever seen was zero bytes.** The
test asserts nothing about restarting; it is a walk that came out right.

**R3: [block.rs:4486](../../../../src/ng/psp/block.rs) — the every-boundary sweep's docstring claims the one property its fixture cannot exercise**
**Categories:** reliability. Max retries over all 837 schedules: **0**. The sibling test's
docstring says so correctly, so the two contradict each other and the false one is attached to the
test. **Keep the test** — it was among the killers of three mutations — but say what it forces and
pin `retries == 0`, which is the fact the sibling rests on.

**S1: [block.rs:4391](../../../../src/ng/psp/block.rs) — the ceiling's error is tested as a `Display` impl**
**Categories:** smells, reliability. Swapping the two fields in the `#[error]` string fails
**nothing**. The only test that makes a reader emit the refusal uses a deliberately corrupt block,
so the promise the variant exists for — *a genuine record this large is read by raising the
constant* — is held nowhere.

**S2: [block.rs:401](../../../../src/ng/psp/block.rs) — "the measured price of not merging is about 7 %" measures a block-size change, not merging**
**Categories:** smells. The two figures cited are the 100 kb and 1,000 kb rows of the same
non-merging cut rule. **No writer that accumulates across empty stretches has been built or
measured**, so nothing prices the owner's ruling.

### Minor and Nits

Thirty-one between the three. Acted on: the memory fixture scaled with the very constant it
bounds, so raising the ceiling — the documented remedy — would make the suite allocate eight times
it; `a_decode_of_blocks_larger_than_the_buffer_is_retried_and_still_exact` is **one** block, not
blocks, and D4's report repeats the plural; three test names claim "every" where the test does
five; `retries_after_a_refill` counts a restart that never got a refill; `a_bulky_record` names the
weaker of the fixture's two properties; the per-block banner's first occupant is a field that
lives for the whole stream; and the ceiling's value is pinned only by an equality that whoever
edits the constant edits in the same breath.

## 6. Out of scope observations

- **Spec §4.1 and §12 question 3 still say the merge rule ships**, so the design authority
  instructs the opposite of the code. `block.rs` flags it and asks for the spec to be corrected
  *"when that document is next touched"* — an event nothing schedules.
- **The new error class has no row in spec §6.7 or §7**, which are where the instruction for each
  class lives and where Milestone F4's `PspReadError` mapping reads them from.
- **The `psp` → `locus_generation` back-reference is unchanged by this range** and restated rather
  than re-filed. Milestone F remains the last cheap moment.
- **`block.rs` is now the larger of the two files by 601 lines**, which flips the one quantitative
  premise of D3's deferral of the `block/` split. The conclusion holds on the two structural
  premises alone; 82 % of this range's growth is the test module.

## 7. What's good

- **1,523,400 fuzzed inputs, 0 panics, 0 hangs.** 1,514,438 refused, 8,962 ended cleanly. Every
  input either yielded records or refused.
- **The ceiling holds against inflation bombs**: over 400 payloads inflating to as much as
  67,108,864 bytes, the reader added **1,200,128 bytes** to resident size — sampled every 2 ms by
  a watchdog, because a reading taken after the fact is worthless once `fail` has released the
  buffer.
- **The fuzz harness can fail, shown twice** — with the ceiling disabled it fails on memory; with
  the retry arm's refill removed the watchdog aborts at 20 s.
- **Every number in D4's retry table reproduced exactly**, all eight rows, along with the record
  counts, block counts, test counts and every falsification row in both reports.
- **D5's injection reproduced**: 11 failures, 3 of them the step's own.
- **Nothing in the range uses `..Default::default()` in a struct literal or `..` in a trait impl
  that depends on the field set**, and `between_blocks` is written out longhand with its reason
  recorded. That is the rule applied correctly.
