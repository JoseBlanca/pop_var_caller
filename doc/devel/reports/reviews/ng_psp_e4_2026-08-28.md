## 1. Scope

- **What was reviewed:** the diff of one commit, the whole of `record.rs`, `chain_ids.rs`,
  `block.rs` and `header.rs`, and the implementation report, commit message and status entry,
  whose numbers were re-derived rather than read.
- **Deliberately out of scope:** the frozen production tree `src/psp/`; the two example targets
  that fail on a missing `ref.fa.repeats.parquet` fixture.
- **Categories dispatched:** all eight, paired onto three agents.

**What the three did.** **29 mutations** applied and run, **14,720,000 fuzzed inputs** across seven
seeds through `decode_record_body` and `read_record_head`, and three independent instrumentation
passes counting which tests reach the new code at all.

## 2. Verdict

**Request-changes. One Blocker — and it is a change made in this commit, which removed a guard
rather than adding one.**

**`gap_from` was made saturating, which turned a refusal into silent corruption.** An observation's
reads go on the wire as ascending gaps. Before this commit, a list that was not ascending produced
a wrapped gap, and the reader refused the file by name: *"a gap of 18446744073709551611 past id 7,
which is past every id there is"*. After it, the same list is **accepted and names different
reads** — measured, `[3, 3]` reads back as `[3, 4]` and `[7, 3]` as `[7, 8]`. That is spec §5's
failure, *"the observation gains a read that does not exist"*, reached from the writer's side.

**All three agents found it independently**, and two made the same further point: the `debug_assert`
the commit offered as the safeguard is removed by this repo's release profile, and the commit's own
diff weakened the one test that wrote an unsorted list (`vec![7, 3]` → `vec![3, 7]`) — a test edited
to satisfy a new precondition on a `pub` path that documents none.

**And the tests did not reach the step they were extended for.** `twelve_records_in_order` gives
chain ids to two observations per record but leaves `num_obs` at 137 — so two identifiers against
137 reads fails the writer's own inequality and **not one of the twelve records ever derives a
residual**. Measured: a probe that panics on the derive path fired in **8 of 241 tests**. The whole
record-level oracle — the skip patterns, the truncation sweeps, *a body stands on its own* — ran
past this step entirely.

**The arithmetic itself is right.** 14.7 million fuzzed inputs produced no panic, no hang and a
peak heap of **10,110 bytes**, with two oracles that matter for Milestone D holding: `Malformed`
never heals when bytes are appended, and `Truncated` never becomes `Malformed` when bytes are
removed.

### Major

**R1: [record.rs:4593](../../../../src/ng/psp/record.rs) and [block.rs:4977](../../../../src/ng/psp/block.rs) — the multi-record fixtures never derive**
**Categories:** reliability, smells — **two agents.** Two further measurements: replacing
`the_live_reads_of`'s body with `LiveSet::new()` leaves `237 passed; 4 failed`, so the `&LiveSet`
threaded through twelve call sites is inert; and reversing the tie-break leaves all 4,778 green.

**R2: [record.rs:631](../../../../src/ng/psp/record.rs) — the guard does not catch the shape its doc claims, and no fixture is in the regime where it could**
**Categories:** reliability. The window `[ceil(num_obs/2), num_obs]` is `num_obs/2` wide, and its
slack is exactly the number of read pairs whose two mates both cover this record — the shape
paired-end data has. **Measured: a live set carrying two identifiers no observation named derives a
list of four against `num_obs = 4`, passes both bounds, and the reference allele gains two reads
that do not exist.** And the guard is only ever tested at the tight top of the window: across all
241 tests the derived length differed from `num_obs` in exactly two, by one.

**S1: [record.rs:2259](../../../../src/ng/psp/record.rs) — `the_chain_ids_round_trip_exactly` passes when nothing is derived at all**
**Categories:** smells. Its "the saving is real" assertions compare two direct `encode_read_list`
calls and never touch the writer's choice. With the derivation disabled it is not among the three
tests that fail.

**N1: [record.rs:208](../../../../src/ng/psp/record.rs) — `observation-reads` is declared under an encoding of a different shape**
**Categories:** refactor_safety, naming. `ChainIdChanges` is *two* counted runs; a list is one.
`encode_read_list(&[5, 9])` writes three bytes and `skip_unknown_field(ChainIdChanges)` consumes
seven and then errors. Only a later reader is driven by that declaration, which is why nothing here
noticed.

**D1: [record.rs:838](../../../../src/ng/psp/record.rs) and [:1826](../../../../src/ng/psp/record.rs) — the new `live_reads` parameter carries the format's most dangerous precondition, undocumented**
**Categories:** defaults, refactor_safety. Both functions are `pub`; neither doc mentions it, and
the doc ninety lines above one of them still says *"These two functions take no running state and
are given none."*

**RS1: [chain_ids.rs:112](../../../../src/ng/psp/chain_ids.rs) — `LiveSet::from_sorted_ids` moves the type's invariant to its caller**
**Categories:** refactor_safety.

### Minor and Nits

Twenty-one between the three. Acted on: the tie-break is asserted in prose and pinned by nothing;
`residual_reads`' advance loop survives deletion; a `body_field(N)` index swap makes a fault report
the wrong field name and passes; the parity citation claims *"the same inequality"* when
`parity.rs` asserts the lower bound only and the upper is this reader's own; and five field counts
in the docs are stale.

## 6. What's good

- **14,720,000 fuzzed inputs, no panic, no hang, peak heap 10,110 bytes**, and nothing allocated
  from a declared length — over random bytes, a grammar putting `u64::MAX` and `1<<40` into the two
  new fields, encoded records under byte flips, truncation and spliced varints, and five live sets.
- **The harness was shown able to fail three ways**, including an injected `reserve(count)` caught
  by a heap ceiling at input 5 — *"34359739906 bytes asked for"* — which an RSS ceiling missed
  because macOS handed out the 34 GB lazily.
- **Two oracles that matter for Milestone D held**: `Malformed` never heals when bytes are
  appended, and `Truncated` never becomes `Malformed` when bytes are removed.
- **The four purpose-built tests are well made**: the guard test damages a byte whose value it
  first asserts, and the fallback test isolates the exactness condition cleanly.
- **One mutation was proved equivalent rather than reported as a survivor** —
  `decode_read_list`'s `into.clear()` — by 3,000,000 fuzz inputs producing byte-identical counters.
- **Every number in the report and commit message re-derived and holds**, including
  `FORMAT_VERSION` still `(1, 0)` at both commits.
